You can browse public information without signing in. Start with the [community calendar](/calendar/) for events or [Search](/search/) when you know a name or topic.

## Editorial methods and corrections

The [editorial policy](/editorial/) identifies Civic Pulse's operator/maintainer,
source and identity-matching methods, automated-content limitations and coverage
gaps. It sets review/correction requirements without claiming that every legacy
record has been individually verified.

Use **Report a correction on GitHub** on a record, or the
[corrections section](/editorial/#corrections), to prepare a report with the public
page, exact claim, proposed change and original dated source. GitHub sign-in is
required to submit; a Civic Pulse account is not required. The link does not send
anything automatically. Reports are public: omit private/unlisted record links,
credentials, account details and sensitive personal material. No confidential
submission or response-time guarantee is provided by that channel.

Public pages update after successful publication, which can be later than a live
record edit. A closed issue or merged PR alone does not confirm that the public
page changed. Material corrections should identify what changed, why and which
source was checked; a dated note applies to that claim, not to all other facts.

## Start from the homepage

The homepage pairs a civic topic index with a detail panel. Choose People,
Organizations, Elections, Calendar, Maps, or Public records, then follow the
panel's link to the full directory. The selected topic is included in the URL,
so you can share it and use your browser's Back button to return to an earlier
topic. On a phone, the selected panel appears below the topic links. Without
JavaScript, the sections remain available as ordinary page anchors.

Search beside the Civic Pulse masthead when you already know a name or subject.
Upcoming dates group events by their Eastern Time calendar date; every event
remains a separate link. Select **Upcoming dates** to open the full calendar.
Eligible election notices use the same published records as the election directory. The Bibb County link opens the initial
county hub. The header county menu opens summary pages for all 159 Georgia counties.

## Appearance

Civic Pulse automatically follows your browser or operating system’s light or
dark appearance preference, including changes made while the page is open.
Change that preference in your browser or system settings; no site toggle or
saved theme choice is needed. Source map colors and document images retain
their original appearance.

## Explore your county

Open the county name in the header (for example, **Bibb County**, beside the
location icon) to choose a Georgia county. Selecting it opens that county’s summary page.
Choose **All counties** to open the [Georgia county directory](/counties/).
Bibb is the default. Your choice stays with this tab’s session across navigation,
reloads, sign-in, and sign-out; it is not saved to your account or synced to other
devices. A fresh session starts with Bibb. If browser storage is disabled, the
summary page still opens, but the preference may return to Bibb on the next page.

County selection filters people, organizations, maps, calendar events, search,
records requests, election reports, and structured turnout where county coverage
is recorded. Statewide information and records without known county coverage stay
available. The current county coverage is limited: verified local organizations
and maps supply local links to people and events; explicit race/question county
jurisdictions and report counties supply election geography. A county selection
is not a complete list of that county’s services or a personalized ballot.
Each county hub and individual record/profile link has its own named subject.
Changing the header county leaves the current page and opens the selected summary;
use the directories or browser Back afterward to resume browsing with that preference.
Admin editing continues to show all permitted records. Public archive pages keep
their stable page numbering across counties; a filtered page may be empty, so
use the calendar or records-request directory for filtered browsing.

Election pages start with your header county. Bibb uses the recorded local
district list; other counties use explicit county jurisdictions and include
unclassified races. To see the full ballot, choose **All counties** in the header,
then reopen the election from the election directory or use browser Back.
A direct link to a race reveals that race even when it is outside the selected
county, without changing your county preference or revealing other out-of-area races.

The [Bibb County community hub](/counties/bibb/) brings together selected local
maps, organizations, people, elections, and upcoming events. Use the section links
to move around the page, open a district map, or follow an organization to its
profile and calendar. Elections appear newest first and may include past elections.

The [Bibb County overview](/counties/bibb/) includes **Research a local question**:
[follow a Macon-Bibb government decision](/counties/bibb/government/) connects
recorded bodies/people, district sources, agendas and meeting records;
[research a Macon-Bibb election](/counties/bibb/elections/) connects dated election
records, contests and candidate profiles with official ballot resources. Find the
overview through **Browse → Bibb County & research guides** or the county picker.
The guides work without JavaScript and identify coverage gaps. Their record
previews are not a complete current roster, live schedule or personalized ballot.
Use the linked official resources for current personal voting information.

Two more Bibb guides cover [property-tax research](/counties/bibb/property-taxes/)
and [districting/apportionment](/counties/bibb/districting/). The tax guide separates
assessment/valuation and appeal resources from billing/payment, without calculating
liability or replacing the instructions on a notice. The district guide distinguishes
seat allocation from boundary changes and connects saved map versions to official
plan sources. Electoral map features are not parcel or tax determinations, and
neither guide establishes that a particular legal deadline or map applies to you.
The hub is a curated starting point, not a complete county directory. Choose
**All counties** in the header to browse without the county default.

**Bibb County at a glance** shows 59 U.S. Census Bureau measures covering people,
households and housing, income and work, education and connectivity, health, and
geography. **See the story in the numbers** adds Age, Race & ethnicity, and Poverty
chart tabs. Age bars use a shared shared scale across all counties, sized to include the largest age-band share for 18 five-year bands. Race bars
use a 0–100% scale, with Hispanic origin shown separately. Poverty dots show
estimates and lines show their 90% confidence intervals on a shared scale that includes all counties’ confidence intervals. Exact
values and source links accompany each chart. Arrow keys, Home, and End switch
tabs; without JavaScript, all three charts remain visible.
Six headline figures also lead into three percentage comparisons with
Georgia. The comparison bars share a 0–100% scale and matching 2020–2024 ACS
periods; they are not rankings or tests of statistical significance. Open a topic
under **Explore the county profile** to see Bibb and Georgia figures side by side.
Each measure links to its official Census source and includes its reference
period. **Age breakdown** has 18 non-overlapping five-year bands, from ages 0–4
through 85+, with resident counts and shares. **Race & ethnicity** distinguishes
race from Hispanic origin: Hispanic residents may be of any race, and the White
non-Hispanic row is a subset, so all eight rows must not be added together.
**Poverty** shows 2024 SAIPE rates for all ages, children under 18, and related
children ages 5–17 in families, with 90% confidence intervals. Each rate uses its
own eligible population; the child groups overlap. Georgia comparisons use
the same SAIPE series rather than mixing it with state QuickFacts poverty data.
**Download the data** saves a CSV of all measures, both geographies,
reference periods, source URLs, counts, confidence intervals, definition notes,
and the source-check date. Expand
**About these figures** for methodology and the source-check date. Population
estimates, Census counts, business statistics, and ACS five-year survey figures cover different periods; survey
figures have sampling uncertainty. These figures come from structured database records when the website is built.
Upcoming public events load when you open the page. All other Georgia counties
have summary pages linked from the header and statewide county directory.

## Search public meeting documents

Visit the [public document archive](/documents/) to browse published document
titles and filenames. Enter a name, topic or phrase to search titles, filenames
and document contents together. Each document appears once, with matching
passages when available. Leave the search empty to browse, and select **Load
more documents** for additional catalog matches. If content search is temporarily
unavailable, a notice explains that only title and filename matches are shown.
Open the document, its original source, or a related meeting from each result.
Automated transcripts and OCR may contain errors. Unsupported or oversized
originals remain downloadable and show when a searchable derivative is needed.
The archive displays a preparation message until configured; ordinary meeting
attachments remain on their meeting pages.


## Find a meeting and read its notes

Meetings with a recording show a **Meeting video** section. YouTube and Vimeo
play in the page, with fullscreen available. **Watch video on the host's site**
opens the original recording if embedding is restricted or the host is not
supported. Playback depends on the video's availability and host permissions.
Separate committee and board meetings may share one continuous recording.
When a meeting has a start time, **Starts at HH:MM:SS** shows the selected point.
The player and its watch link open there automatically; you can still rewind
or seek elsewhere in the recording.

Site [search](/search/) finds meetings by their recorded notes, transcripts,
motion subjects, motion details, and outcomes as well as their event details.
For example, search **FSS-2** to find the board meeting containing that motion.
Choose **Events** to limit results to calendar events and meetings. Open the
meeting result to read the notes and full voting record. Uploaded file contents
are searchable separately in the [public document archive](/documents/) when
those files have been published there.

Member names in meeting attendance, roll calls, notes, and vote details link to
their profiles. Each profile has a **Meeting votes** section with the member's choice,
motion, outcome, meeting date, and motion details. Select the meeting title to
open that exact vote and its full roll call. Use **Load more votes** for older
records. A profile with no recorded choices says **No meeting votes recorded here.**.
Public profiles list public meetings; unlisted meetings remain accessible from
their direct links. Admins can also see restricted meetings in voting records.

1. Open the [community calendar](/calendar/). Use the month arrows to find a date. Select a day to show its events, or switch to **Agenda** for a list of upcoming events.
2. Use **Organization**, **Calendar**, **Category**, or **Search events** to narrow the list. Meetings are labeled **Meeting**.
3. Select the event title to open its page. If records have been added, the **Meeting record** section shows notes or minutes, a transcript, attendance, votes, and downloadable files.

If you do not see meeting notes, they may not have been added yet. For an older meeting, go to its month and select its day; the default event list starts from today.

## Keep up with the calendar

The month/agenda appears before the event list. Expand **Filter events and calendars** to search or choose organizations, categories, calendars, and calendar colors. After the month/agenda controls and before the event list, expand **Subscribe or download the calendar** for feed and download options.

Calendar times and election dates and deadlines are shown in Eastern Time (America/New_York), including daylight saving changes. Election-day hours show when local polls open and close. **Subscribe to calendar** adds an updating feed of public events to your calendar app. If that button does not open your app, expand **Use a subscription URL** and copy the address. **Download calendar** imports a one-time copy instead. Updates to a subscription depend on your calendar app's refresh schedule.

## Search and explore

- [Search Civic Pulse](/search/) by name or topic, or choose a **Content type** to browse without entering a search term. Results can include people, organizations, elections, articles, maps, and events.
- [Explore maps](/maps/) by choosing a map, then opening **Map settings** to turn layers on or off. Use **Search places, precincts or addresses** in **Places on your map**; select a place there or on the map to see its details. Selecting **Show on map** in the directory moves keyboard focus into the details; Tab reaches its actions, and Escape or the close button returns focus to the directory entry. **Copy map link** shares the current layer selection.

On a public map's own page, **Read public layers, sources and features** jumps to
readable geographic records below the interactive map. These work without
JavaScript. Each public layer shows its recorded description, source credit,
notes, feature count and available source-check dates. Five features preview each
layer; **Browse all features** opens lists of up to 25, with ordinary Previous/Next
links. Layers turned off initially are still included when public.

Source-check dates describe when the source was checked, not when its boundaries
or records changed. Boundary versions and representative names are recorded source
facts and may describe a past period. Follow original-source links to confirm
current information. Coverage can be incomplete, and absent dates are stated
explicitly. These lists and the public map share the last published snapshot;
admin edits appear after a successful publication. Restricted UUID map viewers
do not publish readable records into their HTML.
- Browse [people](/people/) and [organizations](/orgs/) to find profiles and related information. Each directory has its own name search. In People, combine a name or keyword search with a **Type** filter (elected official, appointed official, candidate, or other). The default **Officials and candidates first** order shows elected and appointed officials, then candidates, then other people, alphabetically within each group. You can also sort all matches by **First name** or **Last name**, A–Z or Z–A, then use **Previous** and **Next** to browse all matches in pages of 24. The result count and page number show your position. Changing search, type, or sort returns to page 1; **Clear** restores all types and officials/candidates-first order. Last-name sorting uses the final word of the saved full name, ignoring common suffixes such as Jr., Sr., II, III, and IV; compound surnames follow that final word.

Public profiles include readable office history, candidacies, article associations
and recorded votes even without JavaScript. Organization profiles include their
public officeholders and articles. Initial lists show up to ten offices,
candidacies or articles, and twenty meeting votes or legislative choices. **Read
all entries** opens ordinary history pages of up to twenty records with
Previous/Next links. Source, election, meeting, bill and vote links provide context.
These are published snapshots, and missing records do not establish absence,
opposition or a complete history. A service end not recorded is not proof of
current office; article association roles do not imply endorsement.

With JavaScript, each section switches to its existing interactive view after
loading successfully. Legislative filters still group by bill and show the latest
matching vote; the readable history lists individual nonattendance choices.
If enhancement fails, the public snapshot remains readable alongside the retry
message. Signing in retains the live access checks; restricted records are never
added to the public HTML or history pages. A successful rebuild is needed to
update published evidence.

**Sources and verification** distinguishes source publication and retrieval from
the date of a service period, election, meeting or vote. **Last verified: Unknown**
means no verification date is recorded for that view; a recent fetch or site
update does not establish factual verification. Biography source links are the
citations entered with its text, and election source links identify the particular
date or ballot question they support. An unrelated article or contact website is
not automatically evidence for a biography. Confirm current facts with the named
original sources.

Vote histories may omit meetings, motions, votes or member choices. Missing choices
are unknown, not absences or opposition. Legislative histories exclude attendance
roll calls. Counts describe entries available here, not all votes a person could
have cast, and must not be used as a complete participation denominator.
- Open [elections](/elections/) for available contests, candidates, and dates or deadlines. Select a date to open its calendar event.
  Election details use your header county (Bibb by default). Bibb includes statewide offices and known districts covering part of the county. Choose **All counties** in the header, then return to the election to see every race. Search follows the same county setting where coverage is known. A direct race link reveals that race without changing the header selection. Your own ballot depends on your address.

If a search shows nothing, try a shorter term, another content type, or fewer calendar or map filters. On Search Civic Pulse, **Clear search** removes the query and content-type filter and returns focus to the search field; it keeps your header county selection. Some records have not been added yet.

## Accounts and access

You do not need an account to browse public information. To create one, [create an account](/signup/) with your email and a password of at least eight characters. Complete the security check and confirm your email if prompted. After signing in, [Your account](/account/) shows your account details and lets you sign out. A regular account currently gives you the same content access as a public visitor.

**Unlisted** records can be opened with a direct link but do not appear in public listings or search. **Private** records require admin access. If a link says an admin account is required, signing in with a regular account will not open it.

Information may change. Confirm important meeting, voting, or service details with the organization that published them.

## Follow a records request

Open [Records requests](/requests/) to search Open Records Requests (ORR), federal
Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests, and other records requests. Filter by
stage or organization; organization profiles link to requests they receive or
originate. Open a request to read its timeline, fees, supporting files and news links.

The stage describes progress: draft, submitted, initial response, in progress,
appeal / mediation, or closed. Outcome is separate: not assessed, satisfactory,
partial, or unsatisfactory. A partial release can remain in progress. Follow-up
dates are manually recorded, not legal deadlines or automatic reminders.

Fees are recorded in USD as separate estimated, billed and paid entries. Totals
sum each category independently; estimates are not added to bills or payments.
No fees recorded does not establish that a request is free.

Public request listings and details use the latest published snapshot. Search,
stage/organization filters and Next/Previous work within that snapshot. Imports,
edits and visibility changes appear after publication. Signed-in readers and
**Requests I manage** use live access checks. Public detail links open the
published page; **Open live request with access checks** opens the live viewer.

Public requests appear in the directory. Unlisted requests require their direct
link. Private requests are visible only to their assigned owner and admins.
Account ownership and transfer history are always private. Files can be private
even when a request is public. Download rechecks current file access and uses a
short-lived link; a file may become unavailable after a page was published.

Only admins create requests. If one is assigned to you, use **Account → Manage
your records requests**, then **Manage this request** to update it. You can add
updates, fees, links and files, change visibility, and transfer it to another
registered user using their Account-page user ID. A transfer gives the recipient
access to private files; the previous owner loses access unless they are an admin.
Saving records here does not submit a request or payment to an agency.

## Change history

Choose **View history** on an organization, person, election, map, event, office,
service record, candidacy, ballot question, or article to open its live history.
Open a revision to compare **Before** and **After**, with the edit time and the
editor’s display name. Relationship edits also appear in the owning record’s
history: for example, candidacy changes appear for the person and election.
Use **Load older changes** to continue through earlier revisions.

Anonymous visitors and regular signed-in users see the same history: revisions
must have been public when recorded, and the record and its referenced records
must still be public. Edits made while private or unlisted, including publication
transitions containing hidden earlier values, stay hidden. Public deletions can
remain visible; deleting a hidden record does not make its past history public.
Editor account IDs and email addresses are not shown in public history. Meeting
file storage paths are also hidden; use the meeting viewer to access files.

Tracking begins when this feature is enabled; edits before that point cannot be
reconstructed. Imports and maintenance without a signed-in editor are labeled
**System / import**. History updates immediately, even when the profile page still
shows the previous static build. History is for inspection; it does not restore
or roll back records.

## Civic Pulse short links

Shared links beginning with `https://www.civpulse.org/l/` redirect to a destination
chosen by a Civic Pulse admin. Scanning a Civic Pulse QR code opens the same short
URL. The destination may change while the shared URL and printed QR code stay the
same. No account or JavaScript is required to follow a short link.

A paused or unknown link displays a not-found message. A temporary service error
asks you to try again. Link owners can see aggregate redirect counts and the last
visit time, including repeated visits and bots; these statistics do not store
your IP address, browser identity, or cookies.

## Legislation

[Browse legislation](/legislation/) to search bills and resolutions by title or
identifier. Filter by **Jurisdiction** (state, federal, or not yet classified),
**State**, and **Legislative term**. State options reflect recorded coverage;
choosing federal clears and disables the state selector. Sort by introduction
date (newest or oldest) or title (A–Z or Z–A). Filters and sorting apply to the
entire result set, including **Load more**. Controls update results immediately;
use **Search** to submit keywords. Empty results do not mean a state has no bills:
only records added to Civic Pulse appear here.
Anonymous bill and session browsing uses the latest published snapshot; search,
filters, sorting and Load more work within that snapshot. New imports and edits
appear after publication. Signed-in browsing uses live access checks. Unlisted
records remain available only through their permitted direct links; private
records require administrator access.
[Sessions](/legislation/sessions/) groups
regular and special sessions, with links to their bills, actions and votes.

Public bill, vote and session details are published snapshots, including their
related records and vote counts. Use **Load more** for longer sections. Existing
public UUID links open the published page and retain section anchors. Signed-in
readers and **Open live record with access checks** use current permissions;
unlisted direct links and private administrator access retain their existing
rules. New imports and edits appear in public details after publication.

A bill record includes sponsorship history, documented positions, official text
versions, amendments, a timeline, votes and media coverage. Follow a vote to read
the exact question and each recorded member choice. A vote on an amendment or
procedure is different from a vote on final passage. Sponsorship and public support
are separate from voting choices.

People profiles show legislative voting history with legislative-term, session, chamber/committee, bill-title/identifier and
category filters, plus sponsorships. The office and district reflect recorded
service at the time. Votes are grouped by bill or resolution, showing only the
latest matching vote and the number of other matching votes by that person.
Counts and the latest vote follow the selected filters. Attendance roll calls are
excluded from profile history, entry totals, and per-bill vote counts. Their
underlying source records remain available through direct vote links.
Use **View bill details
and vote history** to see the bill's full recorded vote history, or follow the
latest vote question for its member choices. Bills with the same number in
different terms or numbering contexts stay separate. Votes without a visible
bill association are listed individually. Use **Previous votes** and **Next votes**
to move between groups.
Missing choices mean the record is unknown, not that the member
was absent. Voice votes may have an outcome without individual choices. Coverage
labels describe the imported record; official totals can differ from partial or
visibility-filtered member lists. Unlisted records require their direct links;
private records are available only to administrators.

Bill pages put the timeline and votes first, with section links to sponsorships,
text versions, media coverage and other records. Dates use readable month names;
exact vote times identify UTC. Member names link to profiles and chamber names
link to organizations. **View source** and **View history** remain alongside each
record.

On a vote page, **Official result** shows the reported yes/no/other totals.
**Partial member record** describes the imported choices available on Civic Pulse,
not the size of the official roll call. A person's **Voted Yea** label and the
vote's **Result: rejected** describe different things. Cloture is procedural and
must not be read as final passage. On profiles, expand **Filter legislative votes**
to narrow the record or use **Clear filters**. **Meeting votes** is a separate
section for votes recorded in local meeting minutes.

Long profile sections have independent page controls. **Legislative record**
shows 20 entries per page with **Previous votes** and **Next votes**; applying or
clearing filters returns to page 1. **News & articles** shows 10 articles per page
with its own previous/next buttons. Changing either section's page replaces that
section's cards and leaves the other sections in place. Meeting votes retain
**Load more votes** for earlier meeting records.

Profiles automatically retry brief connection failures. If votes or articles
still fail to load, select **Retry legislative history** or **Retry articles**.
A failed page request keeps the currently displayed votes until the retry succeeds.

Public people, organization and election directories use published snapshots.
Search and filters run in your browser; people still support type filters,
first/last name sorting and Previous/Next pages. Organization searches include
public members and roles; election searches include public candidates, contests
and ballot questions. Use quotes for phrases, `OR` for alternatives and `-word`
to exclude a word. Signed-in searches keep the existing permitted live view;
results and relevance order can differ from the published public snapshot.

## Read the published public record archive

Open [Public record archive](/records/) from the footer, or follow the published
archive link from Legislation, Legislative sessions, Community calendar or Records
requests. Choose a category and use **Next page** / **Previous page** to browse.
Individual bills, legislative votes, sessions, events/meetings and public requests
have stable links and readable content without JavaScript. A meeting page includes
available notes, transcripts, recorded attendance and choices; missing choices
are unknown, not absences. Coverage includes only records added to Civic Pulse.

Use **Open live record and available files** for the existing interactive viewer,
file downloads and authorized editing. Unlisted and private records do not appear
in the archive; existing shared viewer links keep their access rules. Published
pages change only after a rebuild, including removals and visibility changes.
Confirm current details with the original source.

Meeting attendance may say **Observed at a ballot only** when a named vote is the
available evidence; this does not establish attendance throughout the meeting.
**Attendance unknown** and **unknown** vote choices mean the source does not
support a stronger conclusion. “Vote Not Recorded” is never counted as No,
abstention, or absence. Recorded ballots can appear before an official outcome
is known. Anonymous public profile and event snapshots update after publication; signed-in
meeting viewers update live.

Public calendar browsing and event pages use published snapshots. Month navigation,
day selection, Today, Month/Agenda views, organization/calendar/category filters,
calendar checkboxes, colors and keyword filtering still work in your browser.
Public meeting notes, transcripts, attendance, votes and video settings publish
with the event. Attachment downloads still check access when clicked. Existing
shared event links continue to work; unlisted events stay accessible by their
shared links, and private events require an admin. Signed-in views retain live
access checks. Public calendar subscriptions update after publication and your
calendar app's next refresh.
Shared event cards link to the calendar copy carrying a meeting record when one
is available, so combining calendars does not hide the meeting details.

## Election results and turnout

Open [Election results and turnout](/elections/results/) from the Elections page,
choose an election and load its reports. **Show earlier reports** reveals how
reported totals changed. Each report identifies its area, counting group, ballot
style, time and unofficial/certified status. Reports can overlap: county summaries
already include precinct or machine ballots, so do not add them together.

Photo/PDF result reports and **Georgia SOS structured turnout** have separate
availability messages. No photo/PDF reports does not mean turnout is unavailable.
When turnout is available, follow **View structured turnout and source details**
near the report controls to its county rows and provenance. This unofficial
participation data does not report candidate results or election outcomes.
Loading and failed requests are shown separately from empty reports; use
**Refresh reports** to retry a failed load.

Turnout uses reported voters, or ballots when voters are unavailable, divided by
the matching registered-voter count. The page labels which numerator it uses.
The **Voters** count remains visible when registered voters are missing, including
a reported count of zero. In that case the turnout percentage is unavailable;
a reported registration count of zero also cannot produce a percentage.
**Ballots cast** is reported separately and never filled from voter-history counts.
Contest participation can use a different denominator. Missing data says “Not
reported”; no reports does not mean zero votes. The November 3, 2026 election can
have turnout reports before election day, while May 19, 2026 is historical. These
are reported counts, not winner projections. Original photographs remain private
for admin review.

## Navigate and search

Use the search beside the masthead on the [homepage](/) to find a name,
organization, place, or topic. Press Enter or select **Search**, then narrow
results by content type. An empty search opens the search page.

The Elections panel highlights an eligible upcoming election. Outside its
callout window, the panel lists up to three upcoming published elections,
including Election Day itself. Dates roll forward in Eastern Time; new or
edited election records appear after the next publish. **All elections** in
the homepage footer opens the full election directory.

On interior pages, **Browse** groups destinations under **Community**,
**Government**, and **Research & help**. Open it by click, tap, or keyboard;
press Escape to close it. Signed-in visitors see **Account**, and admins also
see **Admin**. Select the Civic Pulse logo to return home. Homepage topics
provide the same directory access, while the footer links to guides and the
Bibb County hub. Maps remain available through the Maps topic and directory.

Election detail pages show **Voter turnout** charts when Georgia SOS structured
turnout is available. The charts use the **county in the site header**, just like
the ballot and election reports. Change the header county, then return to the election to compare reported
ballot styles and inspect early-voting and absentee measures. **All counties**
shows separate, labeled county groups; counts are never combined across counties.
If your county has no reported turnout, the charts say so instead of switching
to another county. There are no separate county dropdowns within the page.
The early-voting chart remains visible alongside reported turnout when both
source tables are available; their overlapping counts are never combined. Exact counts, source
as-of labels (when reported) and the separate collection time accompany the charts.
Charts omit source-note lines when the source provides no as-of label; full source
details remain available under **All reports, source details & earlier snapshots**. Each chart
has its own zero-based ballot scale. Missing counts say “Not reported”; overlapping
measures are not added, and no registered-voter denominator is inferred. Use
**All reports, source details & earlier snapshots** to inspect reporting history.
The charts load live and follow the election’s existing visibility rules.

The **Bibb County precinct turnout** map uses distinct, opaque percentage bands:
under 10%, 10–<15%, 15–<20%, 20–<25%, 25–<30%, and 30%+. Colors progress from
pale yellow to dark green, making differences at lower turnout easier to see.
The darkest band includes all rates at or above 30%; exact percentages remain in
the popup and table. Gray means “Not reported,” while a reported zero uses the
lightest color. Select a precinct for counts and report
time/status, or expand **Precinct counts and percentages** for a keyboard-accessible
table. Choose a ballot style when separate reports are available; only all-method
precinct reports are used, never sums of overlapping reports or contest counts.
Rates need that precinct’s voters (or ballots) and registered-voter counts in
the same report. Imported precinct registration counts enable percentages as soon
as the report is available; missing denominators remain gray. County turnout is
never distributed across precincts. The map reuses the public Bibb boundary
snapshot; check boundary vintages before comparing historical elections.

Georgia SOS structured turnout also appears separately on the election results page.
Choose a turnout snapshot or load more county rows to inspect all available

overlapping early, absentee and turnout tables must never be added together.
Display text and numeric values are preserved, unavailable values stay unavailable,
and an absent source time is labeled separately from collection time. Registration
and precinct denominators are not inferred. Reports update live after an import.

### Share an election section

An election has one detail page throughout its life. Before results are published,
each race lists its candidates and each question lists its response choices.
When a county overview report becomes available, that same race shows recorded
choice votes and vote shares, its source status and timestamp, and precincts
reporting (including the reporting percentage when a denominator is supplied).
Candidate names in result tables link to their profiles. Candidates absent from
a partial report remain visible with “Not reported” counts. Results load live;
use **Refresh results** to check for a newer saved report without leaving the page.

Results follow the county selected in the header. **All counties** shows separate
county and ballot-style tables, never a combined total. Vote share describes the
recorded choice votes, not turnout. A zero count stays zero; missing reporting
counts or zero denominators do not produce a percentage. Results do not declare
a winner or change a candidate’s status. **Source reports and history** opens the
more detailed reporting views. Turnout charts and the ballot remain on this page.

Use **Dates**, **Results & turnout**, and **What’s on the ballot** near the top of a published election page to jump directly to that section. A highlighted **Turnout at a glance** summary follows the county selected in the header and shows reported ballot counts with a collection timestamp. These are unofficial cumulative counts, not candidate results. The full turnout charts and precinct map appear before the long ballot list. Missing turnout is stated explicitly; overlapping source views are never added together.

Use **Link** beside a section heading, then copy the browser address (or use
**Copy link address** on that link). Election detail pages support
`#dates-and-deadlines`, `#voter-turnout`, `#precinct-turnout`, and `#ballot`.
For example, [November 3 precinct turnout](/elections/2026-11-03-general-election/#precinct-turnout)
opens directly at the map. Shared UUID viewer links use the same anchors and
retain their existing access rules.

On the [reports page](/elections/results/), share `#result-filters`, `#reports`,
or `#structured-turnout`. Keep the election `?id=…` and any reporting filters in
the URL; clicking **Link** preserves them. Direct links settle on the section
after its data loads, unless the visitor has started navigating the page.

## Bibb school payroll and payments

Open [School finances](/finances/) from the navigation or Bibb county hub.
Choose Payments or Payroll & travel, select a fiscal year and search a reported
name, recipient or job title. Use the sort menu and Previous/Next for more rows.
The totals follow your search. Download this page as CSV exports only the visible
page; expand Source and reporting details for the original source, file checksum,
export filters and retrieval date. Displayed currency is rounded to cents; the
CSV preserves reported precision.

Payroll names link to existing person records when evidence supports the match.
Uncertain identities receive separate stubs marked **Identity needs review**;
ambiguous repeated names stay separate. Source stubs cite the fiscal year and
original row, and school affiliations describe historical payroll evidence.
No school location or current employment is inferred.

Records are not unique employee headcounts or individual invoices. SALARY and
BENEFITS payment entries overlap payroll, so never add those datasets. Missing
years mean data is unavailable, not zero spending. Fiscal years run July 1–June 30.

### Payment recipient profiles

In school finance results, recipient names link to organization or person
profiles. A **Provisional recipient stub · needs review** label means the source
name has not been verified; an organization stub may later be classified as a
person. Existing exact name matches also remain marked for review until checked.
The table preserves the original reported name, even if the linked profile has a
different display name. SALARY/SALARIES/BENEFITS entries remain payroll aggregates.
New organization pages become available after the site's next publication build.

## Person relationships

Person profiles show **Related people**, with the label appropriate to each
profile and a source link where recorded. This is a selected network, not a
complete family tree or evidence that absent links do not exist. Anonymous pages
include only relationships between two public people. Signed-in viewers can
follow permitted hidden records through the shared viewer; hidden links are not
embedded in public HTML. Public lists update after a successful rebuild.

## Cited meeting vote outcomes

An outcome such as **Passed** can be supported by a later cited observation while
the original import remains unchanged. Open **Outcome evidence and original
observation** on a meeting or vote card to see source links, exact locators,
document hashes and corrections. Published person histories also show these facts.
The observation date is when evidence was recorded in Civic Pulse, not a source
publication or whole-record verification date. A committee motion's passage does
not establish full-commission adoption; missing outcomes remain unknown and are
not inferred from yes/no totals or provider codes. Public changes appear after a
rebuild; signed-in/shared views can update sooner.

### Dated officeholder evidence

Person and organization profiles may show **Dated officeholder observations**
beside documented service intervals. These are source-reported snapshots, not a
complete roster or a claim about who holds office today. Exact dates, year-only
observations and unknown dates are labeled separately. Filtering a day includes
exact observations plus clearly labeled same-year context; it never carries an
older snapshot forward. Check the source/page, evidence status, identity review
and confidence. Indexed-copy evidence is not a verified original. Include
superseded observations to see corrections; missing coverage is not absence.
**Download this observation page (JSON)** exports only the displayed filtered page,
with its parent/filter/pagination scope, not a full service history.

### Government user fees

Open [Reported user fees and collections](/finances/fees/) to search an organization,
reported year, fee name or purpose. Organization profiles also link to their fee
filter. Read each rate's units and tiers separately from **Reported collections**;
missing collections are unknown, not zero. Do not divide collections by a rate to
estimate customers or transactions. Authority citations and source-supplied effective
dates do not establish that a fee applies today. Check the report's source, dates,
evidence status and coverage limitations. Include superseded reports or follow a
fee's history link to inspect amendments and unit changes. CSV/JSON downloads cover
only the displayed filtered page, not every report or a complete county fee inventory.

### Reported obligations

[Reported obligations and commitments](/finances/obligations/) lets you filter
payer organization, year or vendor and inspect source observations/history. An
obligation is not proof of cash paid, a complete contract or an unpaid balance.
Read the source's currency, units, dates, grain and coverage. Separate comparison
cards show only matching visible amounts within one report revision, and only when
source review supports disjoint rows. Do not add cards/revisions or obligations and
payments together. Unknown coverage is not zero or misconduct. Exact observation
and typed vendor links retain scope. CSV exports observation rows only; JSON also
includes explicitly scoped report-group totals. Neither export is a complete ledger.
